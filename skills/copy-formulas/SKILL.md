# copy-formulas

Nothing to install. A formula is a shape to pour the person's facts into, not words to use. The words come from `voice.md` and from what the person told you. Pick one shape per piece, write it, then take the labels off.

## Which shape

| The job | Shape |
|---|---|
| A short ad, a post, a flyer headline and three lines | AIDA |
| A reply to a complaint, a page for people with a problem | PAS |
| A before-and-after service: cleaning, repair, coaching, a redesign | BAB |
| A product with parts to explain | FAB |
| A page that must earn trust before it asks | 4 Ps |
| A long sales page or a sequence of emails | PASTOR |
| An about page, a founder note, a testimonial retold | Star-Story-Solution |
| One line for the top of anything | Value promise |

Not sure: PAS for anything that fixes a problem, AIDA for anything that announces.

## The shapes, with one example each

Same business in every example, a small plumber, so the differences are the shape, not the subject.

**AIDA: attention, interest, desire, action.**
> Your water heater is 12 years old. (Attention) Most fail between 10 and 15, and they fail on a Sunday. (Interest) A new one runs quieter, costs less to heat, and comes with a 6-year warranty. (Desire) Book a free check this week. (Action)

**PAS: problem, agitate, solve.**
> The drip under the sink has been there a month. (Problem) The cabinet floor is soft now, and soft becomes mold. (Agitate) One visit, fixed and dried out, and we show you what it was. (Solve)

**BAB: before, after, bridge.**
> Before: three slow drains and a shower that runs cold by the second person. After: every drain clear, hot water for the whole house. Bridge: one morning visit, flat price, said before we start.

**FAB: feature, advantage, benefit.** One line per part, the benefit last.
> Tankless heater. Heats only when a tap opens. Your gas bill drops and the shower never runs cold.

**4 Ps: picture, promise, prove, push.**
> Picture: a Saturday with no bucket under the sink. Promise: we fix it right the first time or come back free. Prove: 212 reviews, 4.9 stars, licensed since 2009. Push: call before noon and we come today.

**PASTOR: problem, amplify, story, transformation, offer, response.** The long form; one short paragraph per letter.
> Problem: the basement smells damp. Amplify: that smell is water finding a way in, and it does not stop on its own. Story: the Reyes family had the same smell for two winters; it was one cracked drain line. Transformation: dry since March, and the smell went with it. Offer: a camera inspection, 89, taken off the repair if we do it. Response: book it here; same week.

**Star-Story-Solution: the person, what happened, how it ended.**
> Dana started fixing pipes in her dad's shop at fifteen. (Star) When he retired, half his customers called to ask if she was staying. (Story) She was. Same number, same shop, her name on the van now. (Solution)

**Value promise: who it is for, what they get, without what.** One line.
> Same-day plumbing for homeowners in the valley, with the price said before the work starts.

## After the shape is picked

1. Fill it with the person's facts only. A number, a name, a date they gave you. A gap gets [a placeholder], never an invention.
2. Write it in the voice from `voice.md`. The shape is invisible when it is done; nobody should be able to see the letters.
3. Read it once as the reader. Does the first line earn the second? Is there one ask, said once?
4. For anything leaving the workspace, run `python tools/outbound-check/check.py <file>` (python3 on Mac and Linux). Then hand it to the person. They send or post it.

## Never

- Use two shapes in one piece. Pick one; a second piece gets its own.
- Keep the labels in the finished copy. "Problem:" is scaffolding.
- Invent a review count, a year, a price or a guarantee to fill a slot. Leave the slot or ask.
- Agitate past what is true. PAS says what the problem costs; it does not frighten.
- Send, post or publish. Draft, save, hand over.

AIDA, PAS, BAB, FAB and the 4 Ps are old trade formulas with no single owner. PASTOR is Ray Edwards's; Star-Story-Solution is Dan Kennedy's. The examples here are original.
