# Letters with stakes

A letter with stakes is one the person needs to work: money back, a repair made, a charge removed, a wrong put right. It has four parts, in this order, and nothing else:

1. **What happened.** The facts, with dates and amounts, in the order they happened. No feelings, no adjectives.
2. **What you want.** One specific result. Not "make this right" but "refund $84.20 to my card".
3. **By when.** A real date, not "soon". Fourteen days from the letter's date is a fair default unless a lease, a contract or the person says otherwise.
4. **What happens otherwise.** The next step the person will actually take. Only what they told you they will do, and only lawful steps.

The truth rules in `SKILL.md` hold here too: every date, amount and event comes from the person or their papers. Missing facts are [placeholders], and you ask.

## Before you draft

Ask only for what is missing, at most three at a time:

- Who it goes to: a name, a department, an address.
- The key dates and amounts, and any reference number (account, order, case, unit).
- What they have already tried, and when.
- The one result they want.
- What they will do if there is no answer, and whether they are willing to do it.

## Shape

```
[Person's name]
[Person's address]
[Date]

[Recipient name or department]
[Recipient address]

Re: [one line: what this is about, with the reference number]

[What happened.]
[What you want.]
[By when.]
[What happens otherwise.]

[Enclosed: copies of the papers, listed.]

Sincerely,
[Name]
```

Keep it to one page. Firm and polite: the reader may be the person who can fix it, so do not insult them. Enclose copies, never originals. Remind the person to keep a copy and to send it a way they can prove it arrived (tracked mail or an email they keep). The person sends it; you never do.

## Dispute

For a charge, a bill or a fee the person says is wrong, sent to the company or to the bank or card issuer.

- **What happened:** the charge, its date and amount, why it is wrong (never received, charged twice, canceled before the charge), and any earlier contact.
- **What you want:** the charge removed or reversed, and a corrected statement.
- **By when:** a date; if the card issuer sets its own time limit for disputes, say so to the person and use it.
- **What happens otherwise:** a formal dispute with the card issuer, or a complaint to the consumer protection office.

Example:

> On March 3 my card ending 4412 was charged $129.00 by your company for order 55810.
> I canceled that order by email on February 27, two days before it shipped, and you confirmed the cancellation the same day.
> I am asking you to reverse the $129.00 charge and send me a confirmation in writing.
> Please do this by March 31.
> If the charge is not reversed by then, I will dispute it with my card issuer and send them this letter and your cancellation email.
> Copies of both emails are enclosed.

## Landlord

For a repair, a deposit, or a problem with the home, sent to the landlord or the property manager.

- **What happened:** the problem, when it started, when and how the person reported it, and what has happened since.
- **What you want:** the repair done, the deposit returned, or the problem stopped.
- **By when:** a date; if the lease sets a repair time, use it.
- **What happens otherwise:** what the person told you, such as reporting it to the local housing office. Do not suggest withholding rent; if the person raises it, say once that the rules differ by place and a tenant group or legal aid office can check, then write what they decide.

Example:

> The kitchen sink in unit 2B has leaked under the cabinet since October 4.
> I reported it by phone on October 5 and by text on October 12, and no one has come to look at it.
> The cabinet floor is now soft and there is a smell of mold.
> I am asking you to have the leak repaired and the damaged board replaced.
> Please complete the repair by November 2.
> If it is not repaired by then, I will ask the city housing office to inspect the unit.

## Refund

For a product or service that failed, sent to the seller.

- **What happened:** what was bought, when, the price, what went wrong, and what the person tried (a return, a call, a chat).
- **What you want:** a full refund, a partial refund of a stated amount, a replacement, or a repair. Pick one.
- **By when:** a date.
- **What happens otherwise:** a card dispute, a complaint to the consumer protection office, or a small claims filing, only if the person says they will.

Example:

> On June 9 I bought a dehumidifier, model DH-30, from your store for $219.99, receipt 0047-2231.
> It stopped running on June 20, and your service desk told me on June 22 that the part was on back order with no date.
> Your posted policy allows returns within 30 days of purchase.
> I am asking for a full refund of $219.99 to my original card.
> Please process it by July 8.
> If I have not received it by then, I will dispute the charge with my card issuer.

## Complaint

For poor service, a broken promise, or how the person was treated, sent to someone with the power to act (a manager, a head office, a customer relations department).

- **What happened:** what was promised or expected, what happened instead, when, and who was involved by role (not by guesswork about names).
- **What you want:** an apology, a credit, a change, or a meeting. One clear ask.
- **By when:** a date for a written reply.
- **What happens otherwise:** taking it to the next level up, a regulator or licensing body if there is one, or a public review, only if the person says they will.

Example:

> On August 14 I booked a furnace inspection for August 21 between 8 and 10 a.m., confirmation 7720.
> No technician came, and no one called; when I phoned at 11 a.m. I was told the visit had been dropped from the schedule.
> I took the morning off work unpaid to be home for it.
> I am asking for the inspection to be rebooked at no charge and the $95 visit fee waived.
> Please reply in writing by September 4.
> If I do not hear back by then, I will file a complaint with the state contractor licensing board.

## Never

- Invent a date, amount, promise or conversation.
- Threaten a step the person has not chosen, or anything unlawful.
- Insult, mock or guess at the reader's motives.
- Add legal citations or say what the law requires. If a lot is at stake, point once to a local legal aid office or tenant group.
- Send the letter. The person sends it.
