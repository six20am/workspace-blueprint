# review-replies

Nothing to install. The person pastes the review; the reply comes back in their voice from `voice.md`. They post it themselves, so a customer only ever hears from the owner.

## Steps

1. Read `voice.md`. If there is none, say so, offer `voice-extractor`, and write plain and warm in the meantime.
2. Read the review once. Pull out two things: the one thing the customer liked or wanted, and their name if the review shows it.
3. Draft one reply, under 60 words for a good review, under 90 for a bad one. Say the point first.
4. Read it back as the customer would. Would they feel heard? Would a stranger reading it next year want to come in?
5. Show the draft in the chat and save it as `reviews/<date>-<customer or site>-reply-draft.md`. Say where it is. Never post it.

## What a good reply looks like

Three real shapes. Take the shape, not the words; the words come from `voice.md`.

**A five-star review** ("Dana fixed our furnace the same day and explained what went wrong.")

> Thank you, Mr. Alvarez. Same-day was the goal and I am glad it held. Call me if that noise comes back, and stay warm.

**A two-star review** ("Waited 40 minutes past my appointment and nobody said anything.")

> You are right, and I am sorry. Forty minutes with no word from us is on me, not you. I have changed how we call ahead when a job runs long. If you will give us another try, ask for me and the visit is on the house.

**A review about something that did not happen** ("They charged me twice.")

> I want to sort this out. I do not see a second charge on our side, so something has gone wrong somewhere. Please call the shop and ask for me and we will look at it together. Thank you for telling us rather than walking away.

## The bad review, in particular

- Agree with whatever is true, in the first sentence. Nothing reads worse than a defense.
- One fix, said plainly. "I have changed X" beats "we take feedback seriously".
- One offer to take it offline: a name and a way to reach them.
- Never a word about the reviewer's character, other customers, or how busy the day was.
- If the review is about a legal matter, an injury, or a refund dispute with numbers, draft nothing. Say: "This one needs the person who handles it, not a reply template," and stop.

## Never

- Post, submit or send the reply on any site. The person posts.
- Promise a refund, a discount or a redo the person did not say they will give. Use [a placeholder] and ask.
- Name the tool or say the reply was drafted by an assistant. Customers hear from the owner.
- Argue, correct the customer's spelling, or quote their review back at them.
- Write the same reply twice. Two good reviews on the same day get two different notes.
- Ask the customer to change or remove the review.
